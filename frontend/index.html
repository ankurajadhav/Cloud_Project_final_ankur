TRANSPARENCY & LIMITATIONS

Methodology & model notes

Definitions used by the current version of Healthcare Fraud Intelligence.

01

Billing amount anomaly

The assessed provider is compared with other stored providers having the same current specialty. Each peer contributes one mean from valid claims whose saved claim specialty matches that peer's current specialty; the assessed provider is excluded. At least three distinct claim-bearing peer providers are required. Active status does not exclude a provider from this historical peer reference, and distinct providers are not guaranteed to be statistically independent.

amount ratio = provider's matching-specialty mean ÷ median(peer-provider means)
amount risk = round to 2 decimals of [0 when ratio ≤ 1; otherwise min(100, 100 × log₂(ratio) ÷ 4)]
Ratio is rounded to 6 decimal places before scoring.

Examples: 2× maps to 25, 4× to 50, 8× to 75, and 16× or higher to 100. Only unusually high ratios increase this component; unusually low amounts do not. A zero peer-median baseline is unevaluable, including the 0 ÷ 0 case, rather than silently being treated as a 1× ratio.

diagnostic Z = (provider mean − arithmetic mean of peer means) ÷ sample SD of peer means

The signed Z-score is displayed for context only. It can be negative, is unavailable when there are fewer than three peers or peer standard deviation is zero, and does not directly contribute to the final risk score. A valid Z-score can still be shown when the billing ratio is unevaluable because its peer median is zero. Amounts mean billed amounts, not insurer payments.

02

Time-window activity

The reproducible analysis date is the latest valid claim date in the stored dataset, not the computer's current date. Displayed 7-day and 30-day counts and billed sums include valid claims across all stored specialties; behavior scoring uses only claims whose preserved specialty matches the provider's current specialty. Each window uses an exclusive lower date boundary and an inclusive upper date boundary. The current 7-day window contains the analysis date and prior six dates; the current 30-day window contains the analysis date and prior 29 dates. The preceding 30-day window does not overlap the current 30-day window. Data-span flags describe date coverage only; they do not prove source-data completeness.

peer ratio = provider matching-specialty claims in current 30d ÷ median peer matching-specialty claims in current 30d
own trend ratio = provider matching-specialty claims in current 30d ÷ provider matching-specialty claims in prior 30d
burst ratio = (matching-specialty claims in last 7d ÷ 7) ÷ (matching-specialty claims in preceding 23d ÷ 23)
sub-score(r) = round to 2 decimals of clamp(100 × (r − 1) ÷ 1.5, 0, 100)
behavior score = weighted mean of available sub-scores using nominal weights 50% / 30% / 20%, renormalized over available signals.

The peer signal requires a full 30-day dataset span, at least three distinct claim-bearing same-specialty peers, and a peer median of at least three matching-specialty claims in the window. The own-trend signal requires a 60-day span and at least three matching-specialty claims in the preceding 30-day period. The burst signal requires a 30-day span and at least three matching-specialty claims in the preceding 23-day period. Ratios are rounded to four decimals before scoring. For each ratio, 1× or below maps to zero; 2.5× or above maps to 100. These are one-sided activity-increase signals; lower activity does not increase behavior risk.

At least two of the three behavior signals must be evaluable; otherwise the behavior component is withheld as insufficient evidence. If exactly two are available, the 50% / 30% / 20% nominal sub-weights are renormalized across those two. Thus an observed zero sub-score is different from an unavailable comparison. The older full-dataset normalized frequency remains descriptive context only and no longer drives behavior risk. This time-aware behavior component is a transparent heuristic, not a calibrated fraud probability.

03

Referral graph

Every stored referral record is an event. The graph has one directed edge for each unique from-provider → to-provider pair; repeated events are retained as an edge weight. Unique in/out degree counts distinct neighboring providers, while weighted in/out degree counts referral events. Self-referral events are retained but do not establish an inter-provider dependency.

SCC = a group of mutually reachable vertices
reportable ring = cyclic SCC containing at least 3 providers

Tarjan's algorithm calculates strongly connected components. Two-provider cycles and singleton self-loops are not reported as rings. The displayed cycle path is found by traversing actual directed edges; it is not inferred from a sorted provider list.

PageRank uses damping 0.85, event-count weighted outgoing transitions, redistribution of dangling-node rank, at most 200 iterations, a 1×10⁻¹⁰ convergence tolerance, and final normalization so provider ranks sum to 1. Referral structure and centrality are investigative context, not proof of fraud.

04

Composite risk score

Billing-amount anomaly 40%Referral-ring signal 30%Network signal 20%Comparative behavior 10%
network risk = round to 2 decimals of [0.6 × (PageRank ÷ max PageRank × 100) + 0.4 × (incident events ÷ max incident events × 100)]
incident events = weighted incoming events + weighted outgoing events

The network component is unavailable when the provider has no incident referral events. PageRank and incident-event activity are each divided by the maximum for the current graph, multiplied by 100, and clamped to 0–100. A referral event contributes an incoming/outgoing incidence to its endpoint providers; this is not the global count of unique relationships.

ring risk = round to 2 decimals of min(75, 35 + 30D + min(10, 2R))
D = unique internal inter-provider edges ÷ [n × (n − 1)]
R = sum of extra events beyond the first on each internal inter-provider edge

Here n is the number of providers in the SCC. Self-loops are excluded from D and R because they are not inter-provider dependencies. Ring risk is calculated only for a reportable cyclic SCC with at least three providers. When referral events exist but a provider is not in a reportable ring, its observed ring signal is 0; with no incident referral evidence the component is unevaluable.

final score = round to 2 decimals of [Σ_available(scoreᵢ × nominal_weightᵢ) ÷ Σ_available(nominal_weightᵢ)]

Component scores are bounded to 0–100. Unavailable components are excluded and remaining nominal weights are renormalized; an observed zero remains a real score. If all components are unavailable, the score is withheld as Insufficient data. Thresholds: Critical ≥80; High ≥60; Medium ≥35; otherwise Low.

All components and thresholds are transparent heuristics for prioritizing human review—not a calibrated fraud probability, a diagnosis, or validated predictive performance.

05

Persistence & integrity

Local records are stored in SQLite. Backend validation and database constraints protect relationships; provider, claim, and referral mutations are performed transactionally with audit events, data-version updates, analytics recalculation, and a stored analytical snapshot. A mutation rolls back if the calculation fails.

The starter dataset is synthetic and imported only for a genuinely new database. Startup does not overwrite normal user changes. Back up the database before changing or resetting records.

06

What this system does not claim

The application does not prove fraud, make causal conclusions, use genuine patient/insurance records, validate the heuristic against adjudicated fraud outcomes, or offer a calibrated probability of fraud.

Insufficient evidence is distinct from a normal finding. Local SQLite is intended for the single-machine project workflow; AWS/serverless persistence, multi-user authentication, and production healthcare compliance are not configured or claimed as operational.
